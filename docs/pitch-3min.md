# SaafRoute: 3-minute pitch

The pitch is built around three words to memorise: **Report, Review, Route.**

## Opening (about 25 s)

"Every one of us has walked past an overflowing bin and thought, *someone should fix that.* But who? And how do they even know? Today, garbage trucks follow fixed routes, and waste doesn't follow a schedule. Some spots get missed for days, and others get visited when there's nothing to collect."

## The idea (about 20 s)

"SaafRoute turns every citizen's phone into a city sensor, and gives officials a smart plan for what to clean first. Our idea is three words: **Report, Review, Route.**"

## 1. Report (about 40 s)

"A citizen opens a simple web page, takes a photo, picks their sector, and sends it. No app to install, no account, no name. An AI reads the photo and tells us what it is: an overflowing bin, an illegal dump, or just litter. How bad is it? It writes a ready-to-use work ticket in **English, Hindi or Punjabi**.

If ten people report the same spot, we don't create ten tickets. We ask 'is this the same place?', and the ticket simply shows **'10 reports of this spot'**. And if it's not waste, say a photo of a person, no ticket is created at all."

## 2. Review (about 40 s)

"Here's what matters most: **the AI never sends a truck by itself.** An official sees each ticket with the photo, the AI's reasoning and a clear priority score. The score is simple arithmetic, so it's not a black box. The official edits, approves or rejects. Humans stay in charge of every decision."

## 3. Route (about 40 s)

"Once tickets are approved, our system plans the trucks' routes for the day: which truck goes where, in what order, so trucks drive less and the most urgent spots come first. We use real road distances, not straight lines."

## Transparency for citizens (about 25 s)

"And citizens are not left in the dark. A public city dashboard on any phone shows what is reported, who is handling it, which truck is assigned, and when it's collected. When people can see their report being acted on, they trust the system and keep reporting."

## Honesty and privacy (about 20 s)

"We're honest about our limits. The trucks and most tickets in our demo are **simulated data**, and our savings figures are simulations, not real municipal results. On privacy, photos have their location data stripped, reports are anonymous, and faces and number plates are blurred in the public view."

## Closing (about 20 s)

"Chandigarh is our first city, but the model is open, so any Indian city can run it. We're looking for one pilot sector with the Municipal Corporation, to show that this works with real trucks.

**SaafRoute. Citizens report. Officials review. Trucks go where they are needed.** Thank you."

---

## Delivery tips

- **Pause after each of the three words.** "Report." (pause) "Review." (pause) "Route." Judges remember that rhythm.
- **Open with the bin story.** Don't start with technology.
- **Demo in 30 seconds if you have it:** photo in, ticket out, approve, route on the map.

## What was left out on purpose

- **Savings or accuracy numbers.** The savings are only simulated, and the accuracy was measured on just 41 photos.
- **Technical names** such as Gemma, OR-Tools and OSRM. Add them only if a judge asks.
- **"Proof of cleaning".** It isn't built yet, so the pitch doesn't claim it. If a mentor asks, say it's the next step: a crew's after-photo and the citizen's confirmation.
- **Revenue.** The pitch only mentions the pilot with the Municipal Corporation. If you want a revenue line, say "licensed to a city, funded by the national cleanliness mission". It's a recommendation, not a verified plan.
